How a note stacks
its decorations outward

A staccato dot, an accent, a fermata, a trill — each has to sit clear of the note, of the tie, and of every mark already placed beneath it. This is how the engine decides where each one goes, one exact silhouette at a time.

Source: songscribe.layout.stacking · a visual companion to the code

Orientation

The page is measured in staff-spaces, and Y points down

One staff-space (ss) is the gap between two staff lines — the engraver's natural unit, so the algorithm never touches pixels. The vertical origin is the middle staff line, at y = 0. Y increases downward, the way screen coordinates do, so a smaller Y is higher on the page.

That gives the one rule you need to keep straight while reading every diagram below: “outward” means away from the staff — upward and toward smaller Y for marks above the staff, downward and toward larger Y for marks below it. Articulations sit on the side opposite the stem, so a down-stem note (like the one in every diagram here) carries its decorations above.

The data structure

Reservations are exact intervals, not fixed steps

The collision model — StaffExtents — is an exact-interval skyline. Each reservation is a building: a single linear segment y(x) that spans the precise [xStart, xEnd] the element occupies. A flat building is an ordinary rectangle edge; a sloped one is a chord of a curve, which is how a tie arc gets stored without being flattened into stairsteps.

This replaced an older model that chopped the line into a fixed number of equal-width steps. The problem with steps is below: two marks that merely share a step were treated as colliding, even though they never overlap. Discretization cannot tell a real overlap from a rounding artifact, and no step count fixes it — only exact intervals do.

The shapes

Every element shows two edges: an inner and an outer

A mark does not collide as a rectangle. It presents a shape profile — its real edge, stored as a piecewise-linear list of offsets that grow outward from the element's bounding edge. Each element carries two of them:

The inner edge faces the staff and decides where the element may sit — it is what gets lowered onto the reservations beneath. The outer edge faces away and is what the element reserves for whatever stacks outside it. They are independent: the accent has a sloped inner edge (its wedge arm) yet reserves a flat outer box. Anything with a genuinely flat inner edge just uses Profile.flat and skips the outline math.

The core query

Clearance: how far out must this mark sit?

Placing a mark is a single question, asked of the skyline: given my inner-edge profile, where is the innermost position at which I clear every reservation beneath me by my padding? That query is clearance, and it is LilyPond's aligned_side distance evaluated exactly — each building intersected with each profile segment, the binding contact found at a breakpoint, no sampling.

Two other distances shape the answer. Staff-padding is a floor: even with nothing beneath it, a mark keeps a minimum gap from the staff line, and sits at whichever constraint — support-plus-padding or staff-plus-staff-padding — is further out. And horizon padding controls how far sideways a mark looks for something to clear.

The subtle rule: horizon padding widens the buildings, never the profile. Fattening the support lets it reach a profile's zero-offset end, so an accent over a notehead still seats exactly where its box would — while widening the profile instead would let it slip closer. Invisible for flat shapes; decisive against a sloped tie chord.
Tier 1–2, step by step

Watch the column stack, innermost to outermost

Note-attached marks are placed in a fixed order, each seated just outside the last. The order matters: the tie is seeded before the articulations — in LilyPond a script takes the tie as a support, so the tie is innermost and every script clears it wherever their spans overlap. Step through it.

Why outlines

Colliding by silhouette, not by box

The recent change at the heart of this branch: scripts collide by their real outlines, not their bounding boxes. Both motivating cases are marks stacking above a shape that curves or slopes away from its box corners. Against a box, the mark clears a rectangle the glyph barely fills — and lands too far out. Against the outline, it seats where LilyPond puts it.

The bigger pipeline

Note-attached is the first of three tiers

Everything above lives in tier one. The full vertical layout runs three StaffExtents layers in sequence. Each higher tier begins by importing the silhouette of the tier below — copyTopFrom copies only the above-staff buildings — so a hairpin clears every articulation, and a tempo mark clears every hairpin, without any tier knowing the others' details.

Reference

Glossary

The terms as the code uses them. Type tags mark the role each plays.

staff extents structure
The StaffExtents object: an exact-interval Y skyline holding above- and below-staff reservations separately. Y-down, staff-space units, middle line at 0.
building structure
One reservation — an exact [xStart, xEnd] linear segment y(x). Flat is a rectangle edge; sloped is a chord of a curve, like a tie arc.
skyline structure
The silhouette formed by all buildings on one side — the outward-most reach at every x. Marks stack against it.
shape profile geometry
An element's edge as a piecewise-linear list of offsets, measured from its bounding edge and growing outward. Profile.flat is the all-zero rectangle profile.
inner edge geometry
The boundary nearest the staff. Decides where the element may sit; it is what clearance lowers onto the reservations below.
outer edge geometry
The boundary facing away from the staff. It is what the element reserves for whatever stacks outside it.
script element
A note-attached decoration placed by LilyPond's aligned_side rule: staccato, accent, fermata, and the trill on the first note of its range.
spanner element
How the trill seats over notes after the first in its range — a wider padding than it uses as a script.
clearance query
The exact aligned_side distance query. Returns the inner edge at which the element clears every support by its padding, or “no support.”
support query
The reservations lying under an element's inner-edge profile — notehead, tie, staccato — that clearance resolves against. Excludes the staff line itself.
padding distance
The gap an element keeps from whatever its footprint contacts. Each value is ported from LilyPond (staccato 0.20, accent 0.20, fermata 0.40, trill 0.20 ss).
staff-padding distance
The minimum gap kept from the staff line when the staff — not another mark — is the outward constraint (scripts: 0.25 ss).
horizon padding distance
How far beyond its own footprint an element looks for support. It widens the buildings, never the profile (scripts: 0.10 ss).
direction convention
Above vs. below the staff, dispatched on Direction. Articulations sit opposite the stem. “Outward” means away from the staff on either side.
outlines vs. boxes principle
A script clears and reserves against the glyph's real FreeType outline, not its bounding box — mirroring LilyPond building a grob's skyline from its stencil.
